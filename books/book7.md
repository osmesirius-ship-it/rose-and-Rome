# Book 7 — The Mirror and the Many (Gender + shared strengths)

In the mirror game, Rose and Rome swapped roles for the morning, and everyone giggled as the crew tried on each other’s jobs. The builders learned to soothe, the singers learned to fix, and the helpers learned to lead, and the mirror said, you can be many things. Rose said, labels are like stickers that can peel off, and Rome said, hearts don’t have boy boxes or girl boxes. They read the rule of correspondence in a simple way: what we see in a friend can be seen in ourselves, and what is inside us can shine outside, too. They practiced saying, you can comfort and you can be brave, and both are real, because every person holds many colors inside today too.
At lunch they told a rhyme that switched its sound each time the speaker changed, and they listened for the echo like a friendly call and reply. Rome wore a scarf and fixed a kite, Rose wore a tool belt and mended a bench, and nobody said those were wrong for them. They learned the Hermetic idea of polarity in kid words: you can be soft and strong, quiet and bold, and the two can live together. When the mirror showed many versions, the crew saw a circle, not a line, and their rhyme looped around, a little trickier, yet still easy to sing. They promised to cheer each try, because choosing your own way is a brave kind of magic for all.
That evening they sat under lantern light and listed things they liked: climbing, cooking, singing, fixing, comforting, solving, and dreaming. The list did not split by who was a boy or girl, it just flowed like a river of talents. Rose said the inner voice is a mirror too, and we can speak kindly to it, so it tells us we belong. Rome added that as above so below, the stars do not sort us, they just shine. Their last rhyme twined three sounds together and still felt clear, because the promise was simple: every child can lead and every child can be held. They went to sleep knowing the mirror of the heart makes room for everyone, today and tomorrow always too.
