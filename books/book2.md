# Book 2 — The Lantern Rule (Cause & Effect + boundaries)

In Hollow Harbor, where the sidewalks sigh,
And the street signs lean like they’re sleepy and shy,
Rose found a lantern behind a back stair—
It was dusty and bent, but it still had some flair.

Its belly was glass with a jaggedy crack,
Like a lightning-bolt scratch running frontward to back.
“It shines!” said Rose, and she held it up high.
“It’s bright as a wish!” said her hopeful inside.

Rome looked it all over. He didn’t say “No.”
He didn’t say “Yes.” He said, “Slow, slow, slow.”

“A thing can be helpful,” said Rome, with a frown,
“And still be the thing that can hurt you right down.
A crack is a crack, even if light comes through.
So we use what is real—not what we hope it will do.”

Junie came closer (she followed like glue),
With eyes wide as pennies—because Junie was new.
She whispered, “A crack… is it mean? Is it bad?”
Rome said, “Not mean. Not bad. It just is—and that’s sad.”

Rose huffed, “We need it. It’s dark when we roam.”
Rome said, “Then we make it a safer take-home.”

So Rome grabbed a cloth and he wrapped it up tight,
Around the cracked spot where it might bite at night.
He tied it with string like a bandage that stays,
Then set it down gently (no toss, no wild plays).

“Here’s the Lantern Rule,” Rome told all of the crew:
“Before you move fast, check what your thing might do.”

“If you run with a lantern, you trip and you fall.
Then the lantern can shatter and cut through it all.
And if you get cut, you might hide it away—
But hiding a hurt makes it bigger each day.”

Rose swallowed hard. She knew that was true.
(Some hurts grow giant when nobody knew.)

Rome pointed to steps and to corners and stoves:
“We don’t have grown-ups to watch where we go.
So we watch for each other. That’s how we survive.
We plan for the ripple. We keep each kid alive.”

They practiced a path to the shed in the dark:
No running. No pushing. No sharp sudden lark.
They held hands near streets. They walked two-by-two.
They kept space by the fire like careful kids do.

That night on a run when the wind slapped the trees,
Junie got wobbly—her knees full of bees.
She nearly went down, but Rose held her tight,
And Rome raised the lantern and slowed down the night.

Junie sniffed, “I didn’t mean—”
Rose said, “I know. But we still keep it clean.”

Back at the shed, with the flame staying small,
Rome wrote the rule big on the old wooden wall:

“Light helps you see, but a crack can still bite.
So slow down your steps when you carry the light.
Choices make ripples, both little and wide—
So we plan for the ripple and stay on our side.”

And Rose, feeling steady, felt something brand new:
A boundary can love you.
A plan can too.
