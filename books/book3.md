# Book 3 — The Quiet House Map (Correspondence + noticing patterns)

In Hollow Harbor, some houses looked fine,
With curtains and doormats and porch rails in line.
But inside those houses, the air could feel thin—
Like something important forgot to come in.

Some days you’d find cereal left on a plate.
Some days you’d find nothing but “maybe” and “wait.”
Some days a ghost parent would drift by the sink,
Stirring an empty pot—stirring and not even blink.

Rose said, “It’s random. It’s wild. It’s a mess.”
Rome said, “It repeats. That is different, I guess.”

He pulled out a paper, a stubby dull pen,
And said, “Let’s watch closely. Then watch it again.”
He drew little pictures: a bowl and a door,
A bed and a window and one circle more.

“This bowl,” said Rome, “means ‘food showed up here.’
This dot means ‘we asked.’ This line means ‘we’re clear.’
This door means ‘we leave.’ This star means ‘safe place.’
This squiggle means ‘uh-oh’—that tight-feeling face.”

They walked through the streets with their paper-map sheet,
Noticing small things under boots and under feet:
Which porch light stayed on when the sky turned to ink,
Which neighbor said “Hi” with a warm little wink.

They noticed a pattern (it wasn’t brand new):
Some houses felt tight, and you couldn’t breathe through.
Some houses felt quiet, but quiet in a good way—
Like nothing would hurt you for asking to stay.

Rose said, “How can we tell?”
Rome said, “Your body knows well.”

“If your shoulders go up and your stomach feels small,
That’s a sign. That’s a signal. That’s not your fault at all.
If your chest feels more open and you can talk slow,
That’s a clue. That’s a pattern. That’s somewhere to go.”

Junie asked, “So small clues can help with big stuff?”
Rome said, “Yep. Small clues are big clues enough.”

They marked “safe” with a star. They marked “no” with an X.
They marked “ask for help” with three little checks.
They drew a “help signal”—three taps on a rail—
A way to say “need” without telling the tale.

Rose looked at the page and felt steadier, too—
Because maps don’t fix hunger, but maps help you move.
Maps help you remember what fear wants you to miss:
That patterns are power. That noticing is.

Rome taped the map up where the kids all could see,
And said, “We are learning. We’re building a key.”

And under the map, in letters the size
Of a truth you can hold when you’re small and surprised,
He wrote:

“Small things show big things—like sparks show a flame.
If you spot the pattern, you’re not stuck the same.”

That night, when the shadows tried to feel like a trap,
Rose looked to the North Star…
Then down to the map.

And for the first time in a long, long while,
Her breathing remembered how to be mild.
