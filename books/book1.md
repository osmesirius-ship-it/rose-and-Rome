# Book 1 — The North Star Promise

In a town called Hollow where the lights stayed on,
But the hugs went missing and the grown-ups were gone,
Lived children who learned, by the age of about two,
That each other was something you held onto.
Their parents were ghosts—not the boo kind, you see,
But the drift-away, forget-you, gone-by-degree.
They’d promise, then vanish, then promise some more,
So the kids made new rules on the cold wooden floor.
There was Rose, small but sharp, with a pocket-sized stare,
And Rome, who could listen to quiet like air.
When things fell apart (which they often would do),
Rome said, “We’ll do one thing that’s simple and true.”
So when streets all looked same and the night pressed down hard,
And fear felt like teeth and the dark felt too large,
Rose whispered, “I think that we might be lost—
Like… really lost. Like the very-most lost.”
Rome tilted his chin. He did not rush or run.
He said, “Lost is okay if you know where you’re from.”
Then he pointed up high where the cold sky was spun—
To a star that stayed still while the others all spun.
“That one,” said Rome. “That one won’t lie.
That star doesn’t care if you laugh or you cry.
It doesn’t forget. It doesn’t pretend.
It stays where it is—again and again.”
Rose blinked back tears she didn’t ask for permission to make.
Her hands shook a little. Her knees felt like cake.
“But how does a star help a kid like me?”
Rome smiled and said, “Watch. You’re about to see.”
He drew in the dirt with a stick and a line,
A house and a path and a star up top—fine.
“That star means north. North doesn’t change.
Feelings can flip, but north stays the same.”
“So we walk the opposite,” Rose said out loud.
Rome nodded once—gentle, not proud.
And step after step, though the cold still bit,
They moved with a knowing instead of just it.
When they reached the old shed with the creaky-door sound,
Rose sat down hard—safe, cold, but found.
Rome handed her water (not tasty, but warm),
And the shaking inside her began to transform.
“What if I mess up?” Rose finally said.
“What if I don’t know what’s real in my head?”
Rome didn’t rush her. He didn’t pretend.
He said, “Then we do the small-true thing again.”
“We breathe. We look up. We choose just one step.
That’s how we survive what the world hasn’t kept.”
Rose held out her pinky—small, thin, and unsure.
“Promise me this—something tiny but pure.”
Rome linked his pinky. “I promise it so:
When the world feels too big—look up. That’s our go.”
Outside, the wind argued. Inside, a flame stayed.
Above them, the star did just what Rome said.
Not loud.
Not magic.
Not flashy or new.
Just steady.
Just there.
Just… true.
